Most of what lives here has never been counted. The record is thin by accident — a canopy surveyed once in 1974, a river reach sampled the season the funding held, a species described from four specimens and never seen again. Absence in the data is read as absence in the world, and decisions follow from it.

vanyax works the other way around. We take what field teams already gather — acoustic recorders, camera traps, water samples, satellite passes — and build the models that turn it into something a steward can act on: where the forest is thinning, which calls belong to whom, what the count would have been had anyone been listening.

Nothing here is finished. Every model is an argument about a living system, held open to the next observation that contradicts it.

Excerpt A

The forest arrives already altered — shaped more by how it was measured than by what it is. Sensors offer no certainty, only suggestion. A transect, if ever walked, appears broken; but each break insists on continuity. There are signs of use: bite marks, burn scars, the pressure of someone else's harvest. No conclusion is evident, but the canopy resists being closed. It remains — held in transit, not as an answer but as evidence of asking.
